#### R L01 (7 pp): Sampling distributions; point estimators; method of moments
- p1: Def. random sample, IID ($X_1,\dots,X_n \sim F$, CB Def 5.1.1); Def. statistic $Y = T(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, sampling distribution (CB Def 5.2.1); common statistics ($\bar X$, $S^2$, median, IQR, proportions, odds, $t$); Ex. Bernoulli sum ~ Bin; $\E\bar X = \mu$, $\Var\bar X = \sigma^2/n$; $\bar X \sim \Normal(\mu,\sigma^2/n)$ for normal data. **Unique:** framing via CB (Casella–Berger) definitions and STAT2003 claim numbers.
- p2–3: **Unique (answers HW 03's "WHY?"):** proof that $\bar X$ and $S^2$ are independent for normal samples. MVN defined by "$\vect t\T\vect W$ univariate normal for every $\vect t$"; uncorrelated blocks of an MVN vector are independent (STAT2003 claim 40); (1) $\vect W=(X_1,\dots,X_n)\T$ is MVN; (2) $(\bar X, X_1-\bar X,\dots,X_n-\bar X)\T = \mat B\vect W$ is MVN ($\mat B$ an $(n+1)\times n$ constant matrix); (3) $\Cov(X_j-\bar X,\bar X) = \sigma^2/n - \sigma^2/n = 0$; (4) hence $\bar X \indep (X_1-\bar X,\dots,X_n-\bar X)$, so $\bar X \indep S^2$.
- p3–4: **Unique:** proof that $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \ChiSq{n-1}$ via MGFs: $\sum((X_j-\mu)/\sigma)^2 \sim \ChiSq{n}$; decomposition $\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum(X_j-\mu)^2 = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum(X_j-\bar X)^2 + n(\bar X-\mu)^2/\sigma^2$ (cross term 0), the two terms independent; $n(\bar X-\mu)^2/\sigma^2 \sim \ChiSq{1}$; $(1-2t)^{-n/2} = M(t)(1-2t)^{-1/2}$ gives $M(t) = (1-2t)^{-(n-1)/2}$. Recalls $\E Q = k$, $\Var Q = 2k$, $\mgf_Q(t) = (1-2t)^{-k/2}$, $t<1/2$; MGF of independent sum; uniqueness of MGFs (STAT2003 claim 43).
- p5: Def. point estimator (CB Def 7.1.1), estimator $T(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ vs estimate $T(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ (underbraced); Def. statistical model $\mathcal F$; Def. parametric model $\{f(\cdot;\theta): \theta\in\Theta\subseteq\R^d\}$, parameter, parameter space; "Important: distinct $\theta$ ↔ distinct distributions" (identifiability); Bernoulli and normal models; Method of moments: $m_k = \frac1n\sum X_j^k$, $\alpha_k(\theta) = \E_\theta(X^k)$.
- p6–7: MoM examples: Bernoulli; normal ($\hat\sigma^2 = \frac1n\sum(X_j-\bar X)^2$, "not quite the sample variance"); geometric ($\hat p = 1/\bar X$); **unique:** zero-inflated Poisson MoM: $\hat\lambda = m_2/m_1 - 1$, $\hat\pi = 1 - m_1^2/(m_2-m_1)$; whether the estimators lie in the parameter space ($\hat\lambda>0$ iff $m_2>m_1$; $\hat\pi\in(0,1)$ iff $m_2 - m_1^2 > m_1$).

#### R L02 (4 pp): Evaluating estimators
- p1: Def. bias (CB Def 7.3.2), unbiased; Ex. $\hat\sigma^2 = \frac{n-1}{n}S^2$ has bias $-\sigma^2/n$ ("holds without normality"); Ex. $X_1$ unbiased for $\mu$; Def. MSE (CB Def 7.3.1).
- p2: MSE = Var + bias² (proof); MSE of $\bar X$; **unique:** comparison of MSE of $S^2$ ($2\sigma^4/(n-1)$) and $\hat\sigma^2$ ($\sigma^4(2n-1)/n^2$), so $\hat\sigma^2$ has smaller MSE.
- p3: Def. (weak) consistency (CB Def 10.1.1), $\convp$; WLLN (STAT2003 claim 55); MSE → 0 ⇒ consistent, via Markov inequality applied to $\abs{T_n-\psi}^2$; Ex. Bernoulli $\hat p$ consistent.
- p4: Thm: continuous mapping ($W_n\convp W \implies h(W_n)\convp h(W)$); Ex. $\hat p/(1-\hat p)$ consistent for the odds.

#### R L03 (8 pp): Maximum likelihood
- p1: Def. likelihood (CB Def 6.3.1); Bernoulli likelihood $p^s(1-p)^{n-s}$ with **figure** (concave hump on $[0,1]$, "if $s\ne0,n$"); exponential likelihood $\lambda^n e^{-\lambda s}$; discrete interpretation $\mathcal L(\theta;x) = \Prob_\theta(X=x)$.
- p2: Def. MLE (CB Def 7.2.4); calculus facts (extreme value theorem; second-derivative test; first-derivative test for global max; multidimensional: gradient 0, check Hessian); log-likelihood; exponential MLE $\hat\lambda = n/s$.
- p3: global max via $\ell'' = -n/\lambda^2<0$; Poisson log-likelihood; **unique:** MLE does not exist when $s=0$ (**figure**: decreasing line, log-likelihood increases as $\lambda\downarrow0$, but 0 not in parameter space).
- p4: **unique:** MLE may not be unique: Laplace location family $\tfrac12 e^{-\abs{x-\theta}}$, $n=2m$ even, $h(\theta)=\sum\abs{x_j-\theta}$ constant on $(x_{(m)},x_{(m+1)})$.
- p5: **unique:** Cauchy location family: log-likelihood can have several local maxima and minima. Uniform $[0,\theta]$: $\mathcal L = \theta^{-n}\ind(0\le x_{(1)})\ind(x_{(n)}\le\theta)$ (**figure**: pdf step of height $1/\theta$ on $[0,\theta]$).
- p6: **figure**: likelihood $\theta^{-n}$, zero left of $x_{(n)}$, jump at $x_{(n)}$ then decreasing; MLE $x_{(n)}$. Normal MLE derivation: fix $\sigma^2$, maximise in $\mu$; "related to profile likelihood (CB 7.5.5)".
- p7: maximise $\mathcal L(\bar x,\sigma^2)$ in $\sigma^2$; MLE $(\bar X, \frac1n\sum(X_j-\bar X)^2)$; question σ vs σ². Invariance property (CB Thm 7.2.10), proof for one-to-one $\psi$.
- p8: **unique:** induced likelihood $\mathcal L^*(\psi_0) = \sup_{\{\theta:\psi(\theta)=\psi_0\}}\mathcal L(\theta)$ for non-one-to-one $\psi$; Ex. MLE of $\sigma$.

#### R L04 (2 pp): Score and Fisher information
- p1: Bernoulli MLE $\hat p = \bar x$; **unique:** Weibull$(\lambda,k)$ likelihood and log-likelihood (no closed form given).
- p2: score $U(\theta) = \partial\ell/\partial\theta$; $\E_\theta U = 0$ by interchanging differentiation and integration (CB eq 7.3.7–8); expected (Fisher) information $I(\theta) = \Var_\theta U = \E_\theta U^2$. ↔ PE §3.2.

#### R L05 (8 pp): Information, Cramér–Rao, asymptotics of the MLE
- p1: repeats L04 p2 (score, $\E U = 0$, $I(\theta)$).
- p1 (second page, numbered ①): $I(\theta) = -\E_\theta\bigl(\partial^2_\theta\log f\bigr)$ derivation (CB Lemma 7.3.11); observed information $J(\theta) = -\ell''(\theta)$, $I = \E J$; iid case: $U = \sum\partial_\theta\log f(x_j;\theta)$, $I(\theta) = n\,i(\theta)$ ($i$ = information of a single observation). ↔ PE §3.2 (PE writes the observed information with a second "I"; the lecturer uses $J$ and $i$).
- p2: Bernoulli score $(S-np)/(p(1-p))$, $I(p) = n/(p(1-p))$, $i(p) = 1/(p(1-p))$, $J(p)$.
- p3: Cramér–Rao lower bound (CB Thm 7.3.9, Cor 7.3.10) with proof via $\Cov(T,U)$ and Cauchy–Schwarz (STAT2003 claim 38); iid form $\Var T \ge g'(\theta)^2/(n\,i(\theta))$. ↔ PE §3.2.
- p4: Bernoulli: $\bar X$ attains the bound (CB Cor 7.3.15 cited for when attainable). **Regularity assumptions (CB 10.6.2)**: iid; identifiable; common support; open parameter space; interchange of derivatives and integrals, smoothness. ↔ PE §3.2 (PE's list differs: adds $I(\theta)>0$ and differentiability, omits identifiability). Consistency of MLEs (CB Thm 10.1.6): $g(\hat\theta) \convp g(\theta_0)$ for continuous $g$.
- p5: **unique:** sketch of consistency proof (LLN on $\frac1n\sum\log f(X_j;\theta)$, Jensen: $\E_{\theta_0}[\log f(X;\theta)] < \E_{\theta_0}[\log f(X;\theta_0)]$ for $\theta\ne\theta_0$). Def. asymptotic variance (CB Def 10.1.9) with **unique** counterexample $T_n = Z + Y/n$, $Y\sim t_2$ ($\Var T_n = \infty$, asymptotic variance 1). Def. asymptotic efficiency $\sqrt n(W_n - g(\theta)) \convd \Normal(0, v(\theta))$, $v = g'(\theta)^2/i(\theta)$. ↔ PE §5.3.
- p6: **Thm (asymptotic normality of the MLE, with delta method):** $\sqrt n(g(\hat\theta) - g(\theta_0)) \convd \Normal(0, g'(\theta_0)^2/i(\theta_0))$; approximations $\hat\theta \approx \Normal(\theta_0, 1/(n i(\theta_0)))$ ("replace $\theta_0$ by $\hat\theta$"); **unique:** proof sketch by Taylor expansion of $\ell'$ about $\theta_0$, CLT for $n^{-1/2}\ell'(\theta_0)$, LLN for $-n^{-1}\ell''(\theta_0)\convp i(\theta_0)$. (Not in PE or CN, which state no such theorem.)
- p7: **unique:** Poisson, estimating $\Prob(X=0) = e^{-\lambda}$: MLE $e^{-\bar X}$, asymptotic variance $\lambda e^{-2\lambda}/n$, compared with the unbiased $\bar Y$ ($Y_j = \ind\{X_j = 0\}$), $\Var\bar Y = e^{-\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda})/n$; comparison with the CR bound ($e^\lambda - 1 \ge \lambda$).

#### R L07 (6 pp): Multiple linear regression; QR decomposition
- p1: model $Y = \beta_1x_1+\dots+\beta_px_p+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$; conditional distribution $Y\given\vect X=\vect x \sim \Normal(\vect x\vect\beta,\sigma^2)$, $\vect x$ a row vector; $x_1=1$ gives the intercept $\beta_1$; data $(Y_j,\vect X_j)\iid F_{Y,\vect X}$; **unique:** factorisation of the likelihood into the conditional likelihood $\mathcal L_1$ times $\prod f_{\vect X}(\vect x_j)$ (covariates' distribution); conditional log-likelihood $-\frac n2\log\sigma^2 - \frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum(y_j-\vect x_j\vect\beta)^2 - \frac n2\log 2\pi$.
- p2: design (model) matrix $\mat X$ ($n\times p$); $\sum(y_j-\vect x_j\vect\beta)^2 = (\vect y-\mat X\vect\beta)\T(\vect y-\mat X\vect\beta)$; normal equations $\mat X\T\mat X\hat{\vect\beta} = \mat X\T\vect y$; $\hat{\vect\beta} = (\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}\mat X\T\vect y$ if invertible; **unique:** proof that $\hat{\vect\beta}$ minimises, by expanding along $\hat{\vect\beta}+h\vect u$ ($\vect u\in\R^p$, $h\in\R$): $\ldots - 2h\vect u\T\mat X\T(\vect y-\mat X\hat{\vect\beta}) + h^2\vect u\T\mat X\T\mat X\vect u$, minimised at $h=0$ by the normal equations ("show $\vect u\T\mat X\T\mat X\vect u>0$"); $\mat X\T\mat X$ invertible iff $\mat X$ has full (column) rank.
- p3: $\hat\sigma^2_{\text{MLE}} = \frac1n\lVert\vect y-\mat X\hat{\vect\beta}\rVert^2$; unbiased $\hat\sigma^2_{\text{unbiased}} = \frac1{n-p}(\cdots)$; STAT2003 claim 45 ($\vect W=\mat A\vect V$: $\vect\mu_W=\mat A\vect\mu_V$, $\Sigma_W = \mat A\Sigma_V\mat A\T$); $\vect Y\sim\Normal(\mat X\vect\beta,\sigma^2\mat I)$ conditional on $\mat X$; (1) $\hat{\vect\beta}$ unbiased (proof).
- p4: (2) $\Cov(\hat{\vect\beta}) = \sigma^2(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}$ (proof, using symmetry of $(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}$); (3) $\hat{\vect\beta}\sim\Normal(\vect\beta,\sigma^2(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1})$ (linear transformation of a normal vector). **Unique:** QR decomposition $\mat X = \mat Q\binom{\mat R}{\mat 0}$, $\mat Q$ $n\times n$ orthogonal, $\mat R$ $p\times p$ upper triangular; $\lVert\vect y-\mat X\vect\beta\rVert^2 = \lVert\mat Q\T\vect y - \binom{\mat R}{\mat 0}\vect\beta\rVert^2$; $\mat Q\T\vect y = \binom{\vect f}{\vect r}$ (dimensions $p$, $n-p$).
- p5–6: **unique:** $\lVert\vect y-\mat X\vect\beta\rVert^2 = \lVert\vect f-\mat R\vect\beta\rVert^2 + \lVert\vect r\rVert^2$, so $\hat{\vect\beta} = \mat R^{-1}\vect f$ and $\lVert\vect r\rVert^2$ = RSS; $\binom{\vect f}{\vect r}\sim\Normal\bigl(\binom{\mat R\vect\beta}{\vect 0},\sigma^2\mat I\bigr)$, so $\vect f\indep\vect r$, $r_j\iid\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$, $\lVert\vect r\rVert^2/\sigma^2\sim\ChiSq{n-p}$, $\E\hat\sigma^2_{\text{unbiased}}=\sigma^2$, $(n-p)\hat\sigma^2_{\text{unbiased}}/\sigma^2\sim\ChiSq{n-p}$; $\hat\beta_j\sim\Normal(\beta_j,\sigma^2(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}_{jj})$ and $(\hat\beta_j-\beta_j)/\bigl(\hat\sigma_{\text{unbiased}}\sqrt{(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}_{jj}}\bigr)\sim t_{n-p}$. [p5 ends with a struck-out line "Also $\hat\sigma^2_{\text{unbiased}}\sim$".] [possible error: p4 writes $\mat Q\T\vect y$ but then the covariance on p5 as $\mat Q\Cov(\vect Y)\mat Q\T$; it should be $\mat Q\T\Cov(\vect Y)\mat Q$ (same value $\sigma^2\mat I$).] ↔ HW 45–50 (normal linear model), CN §9.2 (simple case).

#### R L08 (3 pp): Hat matrix, residuals, leverage, influence
- p1: influence (hat, projection) matrix $\mat H = \mat X(\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}\mat X\T$; fitted values $\mat X\hat{\vect\beta} = \mat H\vect y$, the projection of $\vect y$ onto the column space of $\mat X$; properties (1) idempotent (proof), (2) $\tr\mat H = p$, (3) symmetric, (4) $\mat H\mat X=\mat X$; fitted values unbiased for $\E\vect Y$; $\Cov(\mat X\hat{\vect\beta}) = \sigma^2\mat H$; residuals $\hat{\vect e} = (\mat I-\mat H)\vect y$ have zero expectation.
- p2: $\Cov(\hat{\vect e}) = \sigma^2(\mat I-\mat H)$ (proof); standardised residuals $r_i = \hat e_i/(\hat\sigma\sqrt{1-H_{ii}})$; leverages $H_{ii}$ (determined by $\mat X$ only; average $p/n$; rule of thumb: investigate $H_{ii}>2p/n$); influential observations (large leverage or large residual or both); notation $(i)$ = fit without observation $i$; change in fitted values $\mat X(\hat{\vect\beta}-\hat{\vect\beta}_{(i)}) = \hat{\vect y}-\hat{\vect y}_{(i)}$.
- p3: change in coefficients; Cook's statistic $D_i = (\hat{\vect y}-\hat{\vect y}_{(i)})\T(\hat{\vect y}-\hat{\vect y}_{(i)})/(p\hat\sigma^2) = \frac1p r_i^2 H_{ii}/(1-H_{ii})$; $D_i\approx1$ strong indication of influence; rule of thumb $D_i>4/(n-p)$. All **unique** (not in HW or CN).

#### R L09 (4 pp): Regression diagnostics
- p1: outliers; **unique:** studentised (deleted) residuals: $\Var(Y_i-\hat Y_{(i)}) = \sigma^2 + \sigma^2\vect x_i(\mat X_{(i)}\T\mat X_{(i)})^{-1}\vect x_i\T$; $t_i = (y_i-\hat y_{(i)})/\bigl(\hat\sigma_{(i)}\sqrt{1+\vect x_i(\mat X_{(i)}\T\mat X_{(i)})^{-1}\vect x_i\T}\bigr) = \hat e_i/(\hat\sigma_{(i)}\sqrt{1-H_{ii}}) \sim t_{n-1-p}$.
- p2: **unique:** Bonferroni cut-off for outliers: $\Prob(\bigcup A_i)\le n\Prob(A_1)$, use the $1-\alpha/(2n)$ quantile of $t_{n-p-1}$; mean structure: plot residuals against each covariate; added-variable plot; partial residual plot ($\hat e + \hat\beta_jx_j$ against $x_j$); interaction term $\beta_3x_1x_2$ to handle non-linearity.
- p3: **unique:** collinearity: no full column rank ⇒ normal equations have no unique solution; near-singular $\mat X\T\mat X$ ⇒ imprecise estimates; detection by pairwise correlations, condition number $\kappa = \sqrt{\lambda_1/\lambda_p}$ (eigenvalues of $\mat X\T\mat X$; $\ge30$ suggests problems, affected by scaling), variance inflation factors $\Var(\hat\beta_j) = \sigma^2\,\frac{1}{1-R_j^2}\,\frac{1}{\sum_i(x_{ji}-\bar x_j)^2}$ (VIF $>5$ worth investigating, $>10$ suggests a problem).
- p4: **unique:** changes in scale ($x_j\to(x_j+a)/b$ changes $\hat\beta_j$ to $b\hat\beta_j$; $\hat\sigma^2$, $t$ unchanged); transforming the response: log-normal model, $\E Y = \exp(\beta_0+\beta_1x)\exp(\sigma^2/2)$, $\Var Y = \text{const}\cdot(\E Y)^2$; Box–Cox $g_\lambda(y) = (y^\lambda-1)/\lambda$ ($\lambda\ne0$), $\log y$ ($\lambda=0$), $y>0$.

#### R L10 (2 pp): Box–Cox likelihood; Gauss–Markov theorem
- p1: **unique:** Box–Cox model $g_\lambda(Y) = \beta_0+\beta_1x_1+\dots+\beta_{p-1}x_{p-1}+\varepsilon$; distribution of $Y$ via $W = g_\lambda(Y)\sim\Normal(\mu,\sigma^2)$: $\Prob(Y\le y) = \Phi\bigl((g_\lambda(y)-\mu)/\sigma\bigr)$, density $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp\bigl(-(g_\lambda(y)-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)\bigr)y^{\lambda-1}$ (cites STAT2003 claim 25, transformation of densities); likelihood and log-likelihood in $(\vect\beta,\sigma^2,\lambda)$ with Jacobian term $(\lambda-1)\sum\log y_j$; $\hat{\vect\beta}_\lambda = (\mat X\T\mat X)^{-1}\mat X\T g_\lambda(\vect y)$, $\hat\sigma^2_\lambda$; profile log-likelihood $\ell(\lambda) = -\frac n2\log\hat\sigma^2_\lambda + (\lambda-1)\sum\log y_j + \text{const}$.
- p2: **unique:** Gauss–Markov theorem: $\vect Y = \mat X\vect\beta+\vect\varepsilon$, $\E\vect\varepsilon = \vect 0$, $\Cov\vect\varepsilon = \sigma^2\mat I$; $\hat\psi = \vect t\T\hat{\vect\beta}$ is linear and unbiased for $\psi = \vect t\T\vect\beta$; among linear unbiased estimators none has smaller variance. Proof: $\tilde\psi = \vect c\T\vect Y$ unbiased ⇒ $\vect t\T = \vect c\T\mat X$; $\Var\hat\psi = \sigma^2\vect c\T\mat H\vect c$, $\Var\tilde\psi = \sigma^2\vect c\T\vect c$; difference $\sigma^2\lVert(\mat I-\mat H)\vect c\rVert^2\ge0$ ($\mat I-\mat H$ symmetric idempotent).

#### R L11 (5 pp): Logistic regression
- p1: $Y_i\sim\Bern(\pi(\vect x_i;\vect\beta))$ independent given covariates; logit link $\log\bigl(\pi/(1-\pi)\bigr) = \beta_0+\beta_1x_1+\dots+\beta_{p-1}x_{p-1}$ (linear predictor); inverting $\eta = \log(p/(1-p))$ gives $p = e^\eta/(1+e^\eta)\in(0,1)$, $p\to1$ as $\eta\to\infty$, $p\to0$ as $\eta\to-\infty$; odds $= \exp(\beta_0+\dots)$.
- p2: odds $=\exp(\beta_0)\exp(\beta_1x_1)\cdots$; a unit increase in $x_1$ multiplies the odds by $e^{\beta_1}$; $F(z) = e^z/(1+e^z)$, $f = F' = F(1-F)$, $F/(1-F) = e^z$; likelihood $\prod F(\vect x_i\vect\beta)^{y_i}(1-F(\vect x_i\vect\beta))^{1-y_i}$; log-likelihood $\sum\{\log(1-F(\vect x_i\vect\beta)) + y_i\vect x_i\vect\beta\}$.
- p3: score $\partial\ell/\partial\beta_j = \sum_i x_{ij}\{y_i - F(\vect x_i\vect\beta)\}$; likelihood equations (nonlinear, solved by IRLS); observed information $\partial^2\ell/\partial\beta_j\partial\beta_k = -(\mat X\T\mat W\mat X)_{jk}$, $\mat W = \diag\bigl(F(\vect x_i\vect\beta)(1-F(\vect x_i\vect\beta))\bigr)$; $J(\vect\beta) = \mat X\T\mat W\mat X$ does not depend on $\vect Y$, so $I(\vect\beta) = J(\vect\beta)$.
- p4: asymptotically $\hat{\vect\beta}\sim\Normal(\vect\beta,(\mat X\T\mat W\mat X)^{-1})$; standard errors $\sqrt{(\mat X\T\mat W\mat X)^{-1}_{jj}}$ with $\hat{\vect\beta}$ in $\mat W$; predictions $F(\vect x_{\text{new}}\hat{\vect\beta})$; $\se(\vect x_{\text{new}}\hat{\vect\beta}) = \sqrt{\vect x_{\text{new}}(\mat X\T\mat W\mat X)^{-1}\vect x_{\text{new}}\T}$; delta method $\Var F(\vect x_{\text{new}}\hat{\vect\beta}) \approx [F(1-F)]^2\Var(\vect x_{\text{new}}\hat{\vect\beta})$.
- p5: basic diagnostics: raw residuals $y_i-\hat p_i$ (plots not very informative); binned residual plots (bin averages, grey lines $\pm2$ SE, about 95% within); leverage via $\mat H = \mat W^{1/2}\mat X(\mat X\T\mat W\mat X)^{-1}\mat X\T\mat W^{1/2}$ (depends on covariates and weights); investigate $H_{ii}>2p/n$.
All **unique** (the handwritten notes and CN have no logistic regression). Target Ch 48 (GLMs) ↔ STAT3500.

#### R L12 (2 pp): IRLS; retrospective sampling
- p1: **unique:** IRLS derivation: first-order Taylor expansion of $F(\vect x_i\vect\beta)$ about $\vect x_i\vect\beta^*$; weights $w_i = p_i^*(1-p_i^*)$, working response $z_i = \vect x_i\vect\beta^* + (y_i-p_i^*)/(p_i^*(1-p_i^*))$; $\sum_i x_{ij}w_i(z_i-\vect x_i\vect\beta) = 0$; $\mat X\T\mat W^*\mat X\vect\beta = \mat X\T\mat W^*\vect z^*$, the weighted least-squares problem $\argmin\sum w_{ii}(z_i-\vect x_i\vect\beta)^2$.
- p2: **unique:** prospective vs retrospective (case–control) studies; inclusion indicators $Z_i$, sampling proportions $\pi_1 = \Prob(Z_i=1\given Y_i=1)$, $\pi_0 = \Prob(Z_i=1\given Y_i=0)$ (depend on $Y$ but not $\vect x$); Bayes' rule gives $\Prob(Y_i=1\given Z_i=1) = \exp(\vect x_i\vect\beta+\log(\pi_1/\pi_0))/(1+\exp(\cdots))$, i.e. a logistic model with intercept $\beta_0^* = \beta_0+\log(\pi_1/\pi_0)$ and the same slopes.

#### A W7L1-2 (11 pp): Pivots; confidence intervals for $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$
- p1: review of first-year statistics: population parameter ($\mu$ or $p$), random sample, sample statistic ($\bar X$, $\hat p$); "How confident can we be that our sample statistic is close to our population parameter?"; **unique:** Skittles/M&Ms demo: 23 Skittles, 24 M&Ms, $n=47$, $\hat p = 23/47 = 0.489$.
- p2: §6 "Margins of error and level of confidence"; approximate 90/95/99% CIs for $p$: $\hat p\pm\{1.64, 1.96, 2.58\}\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$ with labels (point estimate, critical value, s.e., margin of error, level of confidence); demo: 90% CI $0.489\pm1.64\times0.073 = 0.369$ to $0.609$.

#### A W12L3 (6 pp): General linear hypothesis $\mat R\vect\beta = \vect 0$; the universal $F$-test; polynomial model

- Recap: linear model $\vect Y = \mat X\vect\beta+\vect\varepsilon$ ($\vect Y$ $n\times1$, $\mat X$ $n\times p$ predictors, $\vect\beta$ $p\times1$, $\vect\varepsilon$ $n\times1$); one-way (and two-way) ANOVA as linear models: design matrix as in W12L1-2 Example 9.1.

- Derivation: $\vect\varepsilon\iid\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma^2$ known; $\vect Y\sim\Normal_n(\mat X\vect\beta,\sigma^2\mat I)$; likelihood $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\exp\{-\frac1{2\sigma^2}(\vect Y-\mat X\vect\beta)\T(\vect Y-\mat X\vect\beta)\}$; restricted MLE $\hat{\vect\beta}_{\text{restricted}}$ gives $\exp\{-RSS_0/(2\sigma^2)\}$; unrestricted gives $RSS_1$; $\Lambda = \exp\{-\frac1{2\sigma^2}(RSS_0-RSS_1)\}$; small $\Lambda$ ⇔ large $RSS_0-RSS_1\sim\sigma^2\ChiSq r$ (stated).
- $\hat\sigma^2 = RSS_1/(n-p) = MS_{\text{error}} = MS_{\text{resid}}$; $RSS_1/(n-p)\indep(RSS_0-RSS_1)/r$ (stated); $F$ ratio $\sim F_{r,n-p}$; **linear-model $F$-table**: "Group" (difference) $r$, $RSS_0-RSS_1$, $F$, $\Prob(F_{r,n-p}\ge F)$; "Resid" (alternative) $n-p$, $RSS_1$; "Total" (null) $n-(p-r)$, $RSS_0$. ↔ HW 48–50.
